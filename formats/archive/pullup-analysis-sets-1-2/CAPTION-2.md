# Instagram caption — pull-up analysis, second set

Pull Up Analysis by Fable and DyeAllPies

I asked the AI to judge my pull-ups like a critic, not like a fan. It counted 11 reps, a 12th that failed — and told me none of them were legal 🤖

Last time people said my form needed work. Fair. So this round I shot from further back, the footage got straightened against the room's own vertical lines, and every rep was judged against the actual USMC pull-up standard: dead hang, chin above the bar, full lock-out, no kipping.

The verdict:
✅ 11/11 full lock-outs, no swing, a 0.9 s pause at the top, 60 cm of shoulder travel every rep
❌ 0/11 with the chin clearly over the bar — 4.3 cm short on average, 1.8 cm on my best rep. My mouth clears it. My chin doesn't.
↔️ my right shoulder rides 2.5 cm closer to my ear at the top and my left doesn't — the one asymmetry a single front camera can actually prove
🦵 my knees tuck 38° in every pull. Not a kip, but a judge would point at it.
📉 pull speed fell 63 %, rep 11 cost 2.8× the effort of rep 2 for the same work, then attempt 12 stalled 20 cm below the bar

The colour on my body is not a thermal camera — it's a heat budget. About 45 kJ of heat from the set, split between muscles by how hard surface-EMG studies say each one works in a pull-up (Youdas et al. 2010) and how much muscle there is to warm (MRI volumes, Holzbaur et al. 2007), minus what the blood carries away — that drain rate and its time constant come from González-Alonso et al. 2000, who measured both directly. Lats and forearms finish about +2 °C. My legs finish at +0.08 °C, which is why they stay blue: they hold the tuck, they don't lift me. Colour is temperature, brightness pulses with activation, so the map breathes with each rep.

Then the part I didn't expect. My chest visibly flushes during the set, so we measured it — the redness index of my chest skin at the dead hang after every rep, with a fixed patch of wall in the same frame subtracted so a change in exposure cancels. It doubles over the first five reps and then plateaus, and it tracks the modelled muscle temperature at r = 0.83. One measured signal on the skin agreeing with a modelled one underneath it. Small sample, one person, still exploratory — but it's real data, not a literature guess.

Two independent trackers agree on the count (MediaPipe and YOLOv8, shoulder tracks r = 0.998). The one thing I won't claim: in 3D my left elbow looks 20° more bent than my right, but measured in the image plane the gap is 2.6°. Model and measurement disagree, so that stays unresolved until I film from the side.

Four centimetres more pull and these all count. Which cue would you give me? 👇
