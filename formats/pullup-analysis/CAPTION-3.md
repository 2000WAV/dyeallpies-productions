# Instagram caption — pull-up analysis, third set

Pull Up Analysis by Fable and DyeAllPies

Third set, and this time I gave the AI a literature review before it judged me: 60 papers on pull-up EMG, biomechanics, velocity loss, muscle heat and skin temperature, all downloaded and cited. 11 reps. Chin at the bar on every one, never clearly over it. Same verdict as last time, from a different camera and a different method 🤖

What is new on the body: 14 muscles drawn as muscles, with fibre direction, boundaries and the frame's own shading, coloured by a modelled temperature over 23 muscles, and this time the heat follows the mechanics: the force on the bar and the levers of the elbow and shoulder give each joint's torque, the muscles share it, and a fatigue model makes every late rep draw on a smaller pool of motor units. Biceps, forearms and lats finish about +1 °C, chest +0.8, legs +0.1 because they only hold the tuck. It is a model, not a thermal camera, and the legend says so on every frame.

The verdict:
✅ 11/11 full lock-outs, no swing (hips travel 5 cm), a 0.7 s pause at the top, head behind the bar for over a second on every rep
🟡 chin 3.5 cm below the bar's top edge in the doorway plane, of which the floor camera hides about 3.4 cm. Adjusted: −1.6 to +1.7 cm on the eleven reps. At the bar. A strict judge wants 3 more.
↔️ my right shoulder rides 3° low at the top while my wrists sit level on the bar
📉 pull speed −40 % from rep 1, −53 % from my fastest rep; the 25 % training cut-off came at rep 10

The camera is on the floor 2.8 m away looking up 6.5°, so every landmark is measured in a rectified doorway plane (the two vanishing points give the focal length and the camera's tilt), the scale is my standing height confirmed by my arm length at the dead hang to half a percent, and the depth allowance is computed from the geometry instead of guessed. Two trackers agree on the count (r = 0.998).

My chest reddens over the set again, measured against a patch of wall: +27 units, tracking effort. Last time I called that muscle heat reaching the skin. The physiology says skin blood flow actually falls in the first minute of exercise, so it is not that. Corrected.
