# Worked example #3 — the third pull-up set (IMG_6108.MOV, 2026-09-08)

Full numbers: [`pullup/REPORT-3.md`](../../../../pullup/REPORT-3.md). Caption:
[`pullup/CAPTION-3.md`](../../../../pullup/CAPTION-3.md). Assessment of sets #1 and #2 and
the plan: [`pullup/PLAN-3.md`](../../../../pullup/PLAN-3.md). This file records how the result
was reached and what went wrong on the way.

Dennis's brief: set #1 (the full-length one) passed 140k views on Instagram; do the next one
seriously — research first, everything downloaded and cited, more anatomy in the muscle
visualisation, more heat, more calculation, a jungle with high-definition monkeys, and a
push-up video in the same style afterwards.

## The clip

63.6 s iPhone 14 MOV, rotation tag 90 → 1080×1920, 30 fps, 1909 frames, **10-bit HLG**. Phone
on the floor 2.8 m from the doorway, looking up 6.5°, almost no roll (0.3°), yaw 5.8°. Feet in
frame; at the top of every rep the whole head vanishes behind the bar and the underside of the
lintel it is mounted under. A black cat sits in the bottom of the frame from 15 s. 11 reps,
no failed attempt, a 3.9 s dead hang before the first pull.

## Timeline of the session (≈5 h)

1. **Research first, in parallel.** Six background agents (two at a time), each writing one
   file into `references/pullup-science/` with abstracts, open-access PDFs and CSV tables:
   EMG and anatomy (22 new papers), biomechanics and velocity (17), norms and energy, thermal
   skin physiology and permissive anatomy assets (Gray's plates, OpenSim models), an ML
   tooling survey with licences, and Reel virality notes. Dennis added mid-way: download
   everything reusable. `DOWNLOADS.md` logs every file.
2. **Critical look at #1 and #2 frame by frame** before touching the new clip (`PLAN-3.md`
   §1): #2's body read as a blue mannequin, its 20 s cut threw away the thing that worked in
   #1 (watching the reps), its text was too small for a phone, and the HLG footage had been
   decoded flat.
3. **Tone-mapped master.** libplacebo (bt.2390, Vulkan) beats zscale hable/mobius by eye;
   the plain decode is grey. `pullup/work3/master.mp4`, NVENC cq 12.
4. **Geometry.** Vertical VP from 100 long edges, horizontal VP from 149 (bar, lintel, tiles,
   shelves); f = 1950 px from their orthogonality; H = K Rᵀ K⁻¹; a residual 0.72° rotation so
   the fitted bar is level (the lintel tilts identically, so the residual was the model, not
   the bar). The first attempt mirrored the image: the plane normal must point away from the
   camera. Measurement happens in the rectified plane; the render never does.
5. **Trackers on the master** (MediaPipe 292 s, YOLO 839 s, torso, matte 26 min at 1.2 fps).
6. **`analyze_pullups3.py`** from v2: landmarks through H, the bar fitted where it is
   (`bar_y=`), the lintel too, a phase walker from the *local* base (the deep passive hang
   had swallowed the whole dead hang into rep 1's concentric: 3.2 s), rep-1 velocity
   reference with the fastest rep beside it, 25 % / 50 % landmarks, RIR as a range.
7. **The chin, four times.** (a) "Head hidden behind the lintel ⇒ chin ≥ 13.5 cm above the
   bar" — wrong: the bar is mounted at the back edge of the lintel's underside, so the head
   is hidden by the bar and the underside together and the true bound is only the bar's
   underside sight line. Found by looking at a rep top at full resolution with the highlights
   stretched. (b) YOLO's nose confidence collapses on reps 1–8 and stays at 0.7–0.9 on 9–11
   with the head gone: hallucinated, not a witness. (c) The RVM silhouette shows the neck
   reaching the bar's underside on every top and nothing more. (d) The estimator that stands:
   the face-axis chin at the last frame the face is genuinely seen on the way up (eyes ≥ 4 cm
   under the bar's underside and nose confidence > 0.35) plus the shoulder rise after it.
   −1.7 to −5.0 cm in plane, +3.4 cm computed allowance for a chin 8 cm behind the bar,
   band ±3 cm: **at the bar, 11/11**, the same finding as set #2.
8. **Scale confirmed by physics.** Shoulder-to-bar at the dead hang = 62.7 cm against
   0.332 × 188 = 62.4 cm. The arm anchor (617 px/m) under-reads because the wrist joint sits
   8 cm below the bar; say so rather than averaging it in.
9. **Reference windows are not interchangeable.** The arms-down window (5.5–6.3 s) was
   still close to the camera (face 24 cm tall); the stature window is the reach under the
   bar (7.8–9.2 s); the shoulder-tilt reference is arms-down after the set (57–58.6 s); the
   ear-to-shoulder reference is the *dead hang* — the reach pose reads 27–30 cm and made
   every top look like a 10 cm shrug.
10. **Thermal v3** (`pullup_thermal3.py`): serratus anterior, upper trapezius, middle deltoid,
    triceps, forearm extensors; lat → trapezius shift at the top; core keyed off hip sway.
    36 kJ over 42 s; lats +1.38 °C.
11. **Atlas v3** (`pullup_atlas3.py`): fibre-direction fields (parallel or fan to the tendon),
    boundaries as grooves with an inner highlight, belly shading from the distance
    transform, irregular thin striations, the frame's own luminance deeper. Ironbow scale
    chosen over the blue one on a side-by-side. Preview on hang / mid / top before any render.
12. **Renderer v3** (`render_pullup_overlay3.py`): 1.12× push-in (the bar must stay under
    the Instagram top band: (365 − 150) × 1.12 = 241), readouts as pills beside the elbow and
    hips because the left panels hid the left arm, muscle names once each, a perspective grid
    mapped back through H⁻¹, the cat pasted as the judge, a three-number card. Fonts ≥ 34 px.
13. **Backdrop.** Commons search via curl (the system Python's certificate store is expired;
    thumbnails need `iiurlwidth`, hand-built thumb URLs return 400), 17 originals downloaded
    with full credits into `pullup/assets/commons/CREDITS.json`, rembg (isnet, alpha matting)
    cut-outs — the ones on branches failed and were dropped — placed on the Mata Atlântica
    plate's trunks by `build_plate3.py`.
14. **Chest flush** measured again (+27 units, r = 0.93 with time, wall flat) and
    **re-interpreted**: skin blood flow falls in the first minute of exercise, so it is not
    muscle heat at the skin. The standing baseline is invalid (different distance and white
    balance).
15. Dashboard, report page, REPORT-3, CAPTION-3, this file.

## Second pass, after Dennis watched it (2026-09-09, 00:00–00:30)

His notes: the face flickered (the segmenter's head class was the paint gate); the biceps and
triceps swapped sides on the upper arm going up (a sign flip in the polygon frame); a halo that
went yellow with time (the look pass's rim glow on the plate); the judge's card should not open
the reel — put the shareable numbers on the left instead, without covering the left monkey;
vertical grid rules at the same weight as the horizontals; keep the cat; and the real ask: make
the heat map integrate contraction and relaxation and how the cost compounds, with a scientific
formulation presented to him. That became `pullup_thermal4.py` and `pullup/MUSCLE-MODEL.md`
(inverse dynamics → Crowninshield & Brand force sharing → Hill force–velocity → Thelen
activation dynamics → Xia & Frey-Law fatigue → calibrated heat → temperature). Validation table
against Youdas printed before rendering; the forearm length the model infers from the hang (28
cm) matched the segment tables, which is the geometry check. Cubic sharing (p = 3) was needed:
quadratic starved the teres major. The shoulder's depth behind the bar is the assumption that
sets the lats' level. Both exports re-rendered and gated; the reel schedule lost its cold open.

## Third pass (2026-09-09, morning): the map still only got hotter

Dennis's note: "there is only a progressive increase in heat", he wanted each muscle to light
up as it contracts and lose heat as it relaxes inside every rep, the purple was too dark (blue
to red instead), and the less-used muscles should warm appropriately. The diagnosis took one
trace: the v4 model *was* relaxing (biceps a_eff 0.9 on the pull, 0.2 at the hang) but the
colour was the temperature, which never falls, and the activation sat in a ±22 % brightness
term that the shading swallowed. Fixes: the colour is now the effort index
`0.9 a_eff + 0.2 T/1.5` (clipped), a blue → red LUT with red at 0.82 and a blue floor at 0.10,
the brightness pulse cut to ±10 %; the elbow flexors got a ×1.25 strength factor because the
generic `arm26` strengths made the pull's demand clip at 1 (the lowering then read as max too);
the trapezius got an initiation drive while the elbow is still straight; the legs scale with
g + a_y. Previews at 1:1 on mid-pull / hang / last top before the renders. A research agent
wrote `07-phase-resolved-emg.md` (phase-by-phase %MVIC, ecc/conc ratios, scapular timing) with
the abstracts, full texts and a CSV archived for reuse, at Dennis's ask. His last notes on the
cut: start where the model appears (hands on the bar, `trim=282`), which drops the title card;
he also pointed out that he only holds the bar there, sinks into the hang with his feet down,
and lifts the legs five seconds later, so the pill now says HANDS ON BAR and the model carries a
load fraction until the knees bend; the title's job moved into a GitHub box
("DyeAllPies-Productions" + the URL) that fills the rep card's slot whenever the card is off.

## The review (2026-09-09, midday): "sure you didn't mistake chin-ups and pull-ups?"

The card showed the biceps' fatigued pool above the lats', and Dennis asked the right question.
The exercise was never mislabelled (Youdas' pronated column throughout), but the ordering test
showed the lats barely above the biceps (1.08 whole-rep) against 1.6–1.8 in the EMG. Two causes:
the shoulder's depth behind the bar was assumed at 20 cm where the elbow angle and the measured
segment lengths put it at 26–31 cm (and the first attempt with the anthropometric upper-arm
length put it at 30 cm at a plumb hang, impossible: use the landmark's own length); and the
biceps carried a supinated moment arm. A second research agent fetched Murray 2000 (the three
flexor arms), Holzbaur 2005/2007 (capacities and strength norms) and Kohn 2018 (pronated
strength), and reported plainly that Murray 1995's pronation ratio and Ackland 2008's shoulder
arms are unreadable behind paywalls, so the pec and triceps shoulder roles were calibrated to
Youdas' ratios and disclosed as such. Every constant now carries its source in the code, the
module prints the ordering / capacity / geometry tests, the closing frame is a references card
held 0.5 s, and the stack shows both fatigued pools. Lesson: run the ordering test before the
first render, not after the fourth.

## Mistakes worth not repeating

- Patching scripts with `python - <<'EOF'` here mangles `\n` escapes and non-ASCII (the
  renderer got a cp1252 byte and would not import). Write patch scripts to a file with the
  Write tool, always `encoding="utf-8"`, and build multi-line strings with `chr(10)`.
- A background render piped through `grep` shows no progress for an hour. Run the renderer
  with `-u` straight into a log.
- `taskkill /PID` from Git Bash fails silently (the flag is rewritten as a path); the render
  I thought I had killed kept writing the same mp4 as its replacement, and the file decoded
  as garbage. `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1 taskkill /PID <pid> /F`, then check the process list, then
  render again. Cost: one full re-render.
- The first "head hidden" bound was a geometry guess dressed as a witness. Look at the
  pixels at full resolution before any occlusion argument goes into a verdict.
- The fatigue rates were "from memory of Frey-Law 2012", marked as such, and the shoulder's
  were the table's ankle row. Three renders and a review were built on a story (the biceps'
  pool leads because the elbow fatigues faster) that the real table reverses. Fetch the table
  before the first render; a marked guess is still a guess.
- The renderer's `preview=` indices count rendered frames from the trim start, not source
  frames; a preview past the end silently writes nothing.

## Fourth pass (v4.3, 2026-09-09, evening): the fatigue that carries over

Dennis: "one rep carries fatigue from the previous rep, so each rep should get hotter, and
when I let go of the bar only then should the whole body slightly cool". Two research agents
(`09-fatigue-recovery-and-residual.md`, `10-rep-to-rep-fatigue-in-a-set.md`, 40 abstracts,
the two load-bearing full texts, two CSVs). The mechanism: peripheral fatigue with incomplete
recovery (Carroll 2017), carried in the fatigued compartment M_F; PCr resynthesis abolished
under occlusion (Harris 1976), the hang is loaded rest. Changes: rates from Table 1 (shoulder
corrected), Looft 2018 rest multiplier, colour = M_A + M_F, FATIGUE TEST print, both exports
extended 2.3 s past the release (source frame 1768), the reel to 27.7 s.

## Fifth pass (2026-09-09, late evening): no lines between the muscles, "by Fable"

Two asks after he watched v4.3. (1) The dark delimitations between the regions had to go and
the colour had to flow into the neighbouring part: the atlas now feathers every region mask
against its neighbours (partition of unity, ~17 px ramp on screen) and sums weight × value
for effort, activation and the striation texture; edge darkening, groove highlight, rectus
lines and the per-region belly are removed, a gentle whole-body bulge remains. First attempt
blurred the striations everywhere (the normalised-convolution extension was applied inside the
regions too); fixed by keeping each region's own sharp pattern inside and extending only past
its edge. (2) The GitHub box said DyeAllPies three times: now "PULL UP ANALYSIS · BY FABLE",
`dyeallpies-productions` (76 px after fit), the URL with a trailing slash, the same line about
what is in it; the title card and the references footer say "by Fable" / carry the slash. Every
line measured to its box; the footer sits at the 16 px floor as it did before (18 px is 844 px
wide against an 820 box). No model change, no new gate events.
