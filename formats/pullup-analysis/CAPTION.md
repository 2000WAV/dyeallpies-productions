# Instagram caption — pull-up analysis reel

Pull Up Analysis by Fable and DyeAllPies

10 pull-ups. 92/100 efficiency. About 9 kcal. Every joint tracked by AI 🤖

I filmed one set on the doorway bar and ran it through a 33-point body-pose model, with
a second, independent tracker double-checking the count (both say 10). Every rep got
graded on range of motion, control on the way down, lock-out at the bottom, hip sway,
pull speed and left/right symmetry — and, with my height and weight (188 cm, 79 kg),
a rough energy cost and an effort score.

What the numbers said:
✅ 8 clean chin-at-bar reps, 10 of 10 full hangs at the bottom, ~4 cm of body sway — strict
⚡ ~414 J of work per rep, 350 W best pull, peak ~850 W
📉 pull speed dropped 46 % from rep 3 to rep 10 — rep 10 took 1.7× the effort of rep 3 for the same work
🔥 ~0.9 kcal per rep, ~9 kcal for the set (rough model, not a measurement)
↔️ my left elbow reads ~16° more bent than my right at the top on every rep — some of that is the camera angle, some might be me

The model also caught that I stand holding the bar for 5 s before my feet leave the
floor — the arms lengthen 9 % and the shoulders sink 6 cm the moment the weight goes on.

The heat map on my body is not a thermal camera: it's where surface-EMG studies say a
pull-up works (lats ~124 % MVIC, biceps 78, infraspinatus 75, traps 52, pecs 44,
obliques 33 — Youdas et al. 2010, J Strength Cond Res; concentric > eccentric from
Dickie et al. 2017, J Electromyogr Kinesiol), warmed up by how hard each of my reps
actually was — and the whole body warms through the set with my measured speed loss,
because velocity loss tracks metabolic fatigue and doesn't recover between reps
(Sánchez-Medina & González-Badillo 2011, Med Sci Sports Exerc). Body outline by Robust
Video Matting (Lin et al. 2022).
