# Worked example #2 — the second pull-up set (IMG_5990.MOV, 2026-09-06/07)

Full numbers: [`pullup/REPORT-2.md`](../../../../pullup/REPORT-2.md). Caption:
[`pullup/CAPTION-2.md`](../../../../pullup/CAPTION-2.md). Plan Fable wrote for Opus:
[`pullup/PLAN-IMG_5990.md`](../../../../pullup/PLAN-IMG_5990.md). This file records how the
result was reached and what went wrong on the way.

Dennis's brief: same deliverables as the first video, but faster between sets, "get me
straight", a real asymmetry analysis (the shot is frontal), muscle heating that reflects
physiology, better-localised muscle regions, a critical technique assessment "even a
critical gym bro would see the good in", and a Reel under 20 s.

## The clip

67.4 s iPhone MOV, rotation tag −90 → 1080×1920 upright, 30 fps, 2022 frames. Phone on the
floor further back and higher than the first shoot, so **the feet are in frame at the start
and the head stays in frame at the top of every rep** — that is what lets this analysis
measure the chin directly instead of inferring it. 12 attempts: 11 reps and a failure.

## Timeline of the session (≈4 h, most of it renders)

1. **Camera geometry, and the trap in it.** The doorway verticals lean 2.4–2.9° but the bar
   reads level, and the floor tiles tilt +4.9° the other way. Those cannot all be roll. The
   resolution: the camera has **roll and yaw**. Roll was fixed from the vertical vanishing
   point (RANSAC over 102 long near-vertical LSD segments, VP at (2302, −34747), which puts
   a true vertical at −2.64° where his body is) and cross-checked against three body-based
   gravity references that agree: standing ankle→eye +2.1°, hanging grip→hip +2.8°,
   grip→ankle +3.5°. **The bar only looks level because horizontal lines converge under
   yaw.** Rotate +2.70°; verticals then read −0.03°.
2. **The straightened master** `pullup/work2/straight.mp4`: `cv2.warpAffine` with
   `BORDER_REPLICATE` and scale 1.0 (no zoom, no black corners, nothing cropped), NVENC
   cq 12, audio copied. Everything downstream runs on it.
   **Watch the sign:** `cv2.getRotationMatrix2D(c, +angle, 1)` rotates counter-clockwise.
   The first attempt passed `-ROLL` and doubled the tilt to −5.2°; caught by re-measuring
   the verticals on the output, which is the only check that matters here.
3. Trackers re-run on the straightened clip (MediaPipe heavy 252 s, YOLOv8m 533 s, torso,
   then the matte at 2.3 fps ≈ 15 min). Both trackers find the same 12 attempts; shoulder
   tracks correlate r = 0.998 across the set.
4. **`analyze_pullups2.py`** written beside the original (the first video's numbers must
   stay reproducible). Feet-based hang onset, position-based phase walker, stature scale,
   bar-as-a-line, chin from the face axis, attempts vs reps, the asymmetry suite, the
   technique card. See the rules in `SKILL.md`.
5. **The chin, three times.** First pass used a fixed nose-to-chin drop measured standing:
   it said every rep was 5–10 cm short and marked 11 of 12 attempts "failed". A full-res
   crop at a rep top with the bar line and a centimetre ruler drawn on it showed why — at
   the top his head is tilted right back, so the chin sits close under the mouth rather
   than a fixed distance below the nose. Switching to `chin = mouth + 0.60 × (mouth − eye
   midpoint)` (down the face axis, so head pitch and foreshortening cancel) gave −1.8 to
   −6.9 cm, which agrees with the independent shoulder-anchored estimate to about 1 cm.
   **Look at the pixels before believing a landmark-derived number.**
6. **The bar fit was validated before the headline claim went out**: 1626 sub-pixel edge
   points over 14 empty frames, residual 0.25 px, and it agrees with the in-script fit to
   0.2 cm. The claim "every rep came up short" rests on it, so it had to be checked.
7. **The kip flag was wrong and got fixed by watching the video.** The first rule flagged
   `kip = true` because the hip *angle* swings 38° in each pull. A frame strip through reps
   3 and 10 shows no arch-to-hollow swing at all — his hips travel 4 cm — but his knees do
   tuck up as he pulls. A kip is a swing, so the flag became `hip_sway > 8 cm` and the leg
   tuck is reported separately as its own borderline line on the card.
8. **`pullup_thermal.py`**: the heat budget (see SKILL.md). It lands at 44.9 kJ of heat,
   which matches the independent per-rep figure in the analysis JSON (44.6 kJ) — a useful
   accident that catches sign errors.
9. **`pullup_atlas.py`**: polygons in a canonical body frame warped by the landmarks and
   clipped to the silhouette. Checked by drawing the label map over three frames (hang,
   mid-pull, top) before any render.
10. **The look pass was desaturating his legs.** `LookPass` treats everything outside the
    mask it is given as background; it was being given the *paint* region (which excludes
    head, legs and shorts), so those went grey. It needs the **person alpha**.
11. **The Reel by frame schedule** (`schedule=`), never by re-timing a finished render.
    Cold open on the failed attempt, rep 1 at real speed, reps 2–11 at ×6 with the live
    readouts dropped and a "×6" pill, the failure at real speed, then the judge's card.
    19.74 s. First cut came out at 21.1 s and was trimmed segment by segment.
12. Flicker gate taught about trims (`offset=`) and schedules (`map=`, `skip=`); the one
    event it flagged was him letting go of the bar, confirmed by eye.
13. Dashboard (12 panels), report, caption, HTML page published as an Artifact.

## Round two, on Dennis's notes mid-session

He asked for five more things while the first render was going, so it was killed and redone:

14. **Legs painted.** They needed to be in the thermal table to be painted, and they had to
    be cold to be honest. Splitting the budget did it: the work heat goes only to the
    muscles that lift the body, the legs get a slice of the isometric term. +0.04 to
    +0.19 °C, so they read blue.
15. **Heat that moves with the movement.** Temperature only accumulates, which is correct
    and static. Added a per-frame activation (EMG phase ordering x measured pull speed) on
    a second channel: colour stays temperature, brightness pulses with activation.
16. **Jungle with monkeys.** The existing CC BY canopy plus a capuchin (CC0) and a langur
    (CC BY) cut out with GrabCut and placed clear of the centre column. ShareAlike
    candidates were rejected on purpose - one would push the whole Reel into ShareAlike.
    The first GrabCut used a loose rectangle and left visible boxes of background; a tight
    rectangle around the animal fixed it.
17. **Chest redness measured.** The best thing to come out of the round: a signal from the
    pixels rather than the literature, r = 0.83 with the modelled muscle temperature.
    See the rule in SKILL.md for the controls that make it defensible.
18. **A see-through form grid.** Rules parallel to the bar every 10 cm, a plumb line through
    the grip centre, and the shoulder line against a true horizontal.

Also fixed in this round: the head gate was scaled by the ear separation, so when he tilted
his head back at the top the gate collapsed and the paint ran over his face; and the look
pass was being handed the paint region instead of the person alpha, so it desaturated his
head, legs and shorts.

He also confirmed the camera was tilted and that the floor and doorframe are the reliable
references - which is what the vertical vanishing point had already been fitted to.

## What to do differently next time

- A second camera from the side would settle the elbow question (3D says 20° apart, the
  image plane says 2.6°) and would make the leg tuck measurable rather than arguable.
- Ask for the chin criterion before rendering: everything downstream keys off it.
- The matte pass is the long pole (15 min). Start it the moment the straightened master
  exists, not after the analysis is finished.
