## Post text

26 words. Same frame, only the vowel moves. 19 came back A — four didn't.

People keep asking how these are made, so:

🌊 The 3D waves are not decoration. That's the real spectrum of each word, sliced ~30
times and stacked receding into depth — a "waterfall" plot, how speech labs drew
spectrograms before heatmaps existed. Bright ridges = the vowel. Dim ones = the
consonants around it. The blue and orange dots sit on the actual peaks: those are F1 and
F2, the two resonances of my vocal tract that decide which vowel you hear.

📐 F1/F2 measured in Praat (LPC, 5 kHz ceiling for a male voice), taken from the voiced
core of each word. The target is the mean for male RP speakers in Deterding 1997 (Journal
of the IPA) — 5 BBC broadcasters, citation forms. The grade is simply how many standard
deviations off I landed.

🎬 Cut in ffmpeg against the song's beat grid (109.94 BPM, one word per beat). Every
number on screen came off my own audio.

Where I failed:
• "hot" — F1 693 against a 593 target. I said hot, I produced something closer to "hart".
• "pert" and "hurt" — /ɜː/ is my worst vowel. "bird" got an A though, so it's the word,
  not the sound.
• "boot" — my /uː/ is fronted. That one isn't really an error: British speakers have been
  fronting it for decades. I'm drifting exactly the way the research says my generation
  drifts.

Which word should I run next?
