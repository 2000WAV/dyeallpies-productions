=== KEY NUMBER for 02-pushup-hand-force-and-joint-moments.md: TOP vs BOTTOM %BW ===
This is the archive's most directly comparable ISOMETRIC (static-hold, force-plate) top-vs-bottom
measurement, with separate male and female values:
  men:   UP (top, elbows locked)  71.95 +/- 3.09 %BM;  DOWN (bottom) 76.70 +/- 2.56 %BM
  women: UP (top)                 66.26 +/- 2.63 %BM;  DOWN (bottom) 73.14 +/- 3.14 %BM
i.e. roughly 72% BW at the top and 77% BW at the bottom for men in a standard (static) push-up hold
-- close to, but somewhat higher than, the Gouvali & Boudolos (2005) figure of 66.4% (their "initial
load," a single value not split top/bottom) and noticeably higher than the secondhand Ebben 2011
figure of 64% (standard, unspecified position) cited via adams2022-pmid35992503.txt. The three
studies used different methods (Eckel: isometric hold with a device cueing exact upper-arm angle;
Gouvali: dynamic push-up with force plate; Ebben: dynamic push-up, GRF coefficient) and different
populations -- report the RANGE (roughly 64-77% BW at the top, 73-77% at the bottom, across studies)
rather than a single number, and note Eckel's is the only one of the three with a clean top/bottom
split by sex.

Also: this paper's own Discussion (citing Mier, Amasay, Capehart et al. 2014, Int J Exerc Sci
7:161-168 -- see mier2014-CITATION-ONLY.txt) states: "the percentage of body mass supported is
~75% body mass in the down position and ~69% in the up position" as a synthesis of Mier 2014 and
another citation (ref 20 in Eckel's own numbering, not independently resolved this session) --
close to, but not identical to, Eckel's own measured 76.70%/71.95% (men) figures above.
