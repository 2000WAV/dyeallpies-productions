Yang J, Christophi CA, Farioli A, Baur DM, Moffatt S, Zollinger TW, Kales SN (2019). Association
Between Push-up Exercise Capacity and Future Cardiovascular Events Among Active Adult Men. JAMA
Netw Open 2(2):e188341. PMID 30768197. PMCID PMC6484614. Open access.

Retrospective longitudinal cohort, male firefighters (Indiana), baseline exams 2000-2007,
followed through 2010. 1562 enrolled, 1104 with usable push-up data, mean age 39.6 (SD 9.2), mean
BMI 28.7 (SD 4.3). Push-up capacity split into 5 categories (arbitrary, 10-rep bins):
  category 1: 0-10 push-ups
  category 2: 11-20 push-ups
  category 3: 21-30 push-ups
  category 4: 31-40 push-ups
  category 5: >=41 push-ups

37 CVD-related outcomes over 8601 person-years, 10-y follow-up. Incidence rate ratio (IRR) table,
category 1 (0-10 push-ups) as reference:

  category            events   rate per 100,000 person-yr   IRR (95% CI)
  0-10  (ref)            8              1757                 1 [Reference]
  11-20                  9               625                 0.36 (0.14-0.92)
  21-30                  9               288                 0.16 (0.06-0.42)
  31-40                 10               433                 0.25 (0.10-0.62)
  >=41                   1                79                 0.04 (0.01-0.36)

Headline comparison (also in the abstract): participants completing >40 push-ups had significantly
lower CVD-event risk than those completing <10 push-ups (IRR 0.04, 95% CI 0.01-0.36).

Cox proportional-hazards models (age+BMI adjusted) partially attenuated but did not eliminate the
association; hazard ratios for push-up category 5 vs 1 in the adjusted model: HR 0.13 (0.01-1.14,
unadjusted-for-VO2max) / 0.11 (0.01-1.07, VO2max-adjusted too) -- both borderline (p=.06-.07),
category 3 (21-30) remained significant after full adjustment (HR 0.25, 0.08-0.76, p=.01).

CAVEATS for use in the pipeline: (1) this is an OBSERVATIONAL cohort of a physically active,
occupationally-selected population (career firefighters, not the general public) -- do not
generalize the exact IRR magnitudes to a general/untrained population; (2) push-up TEST PROTOCOL
(cadence, rest, form standard) is not detailed in the extract obtained this session -- a future
session should pull the Methods section in full if the exact test protocol matters; (3) this is an
association, not a causal claim -- higher push-up capacity is very likely a marker of general
fitness/leanness/younger relative age within the cohort rather than a proof that push-ups themselves
prevent CVD events.
