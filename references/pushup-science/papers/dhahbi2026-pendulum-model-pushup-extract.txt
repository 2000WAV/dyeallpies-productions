Dhahbi W (2026). A Rigid-Body Pendulum Model for Plyometric Push-Up Biomechanics: Analytical
Derivation and Numerical Quantification of Flight Time, Arc Displacement, Maximum Height, and
Mechanical Power Output. Bioengineering (Basel) 13(4):445. doi:10.3390/bioengineering13040445.
PMID 42072239. PMCID PMC13113941. CC BY 4.0, open access (MDPI). Single-author analytical/
numerical-simulation paper (no human subjects) -- the "recent (2019-2026) inverse-dynamics-style
push-up model" requested for this file.

WHAT IT DOES: models the plyometric (clap/box-drop) push-up's flight phase as a single-link rigid
body pivoting about the ankle (planar pendulum), instead of the conventional simplification that
treats the whole body as a point mass in free-fall (used by essentially every prior GRF-based
plyometric push-up study, including moore2012, dhahbi2017, garciamasso2011, zalleg2020 in this
same folder -- none of which correct for this). Derives the "effective pendulum length"
L = (M_W / M) x L_OS from two independent routes (static moment equilibrium; gravitational-torque
coordinate approach) and shows they agree (deviation from the naive M_W-to-CoM distance is
< 4% for takeoff angles theta_0 <= 16 degrees, which covers most adult push-up configurations).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULT (source of the number used in 02-pushup-hand-force-and-joint-moments.md):
- The conventional free-fall model systematically OVERESTIMATES flight time by up to 18.82%
  (delta t = 0.096 s at pendulum-arm length L_OW = 0.50 m, initial hand velocity V_H,0 = 2.50 m/s)
  and OVERESTIMATES maximum height by up to 28.43% (delta h = 0.087 m), relative to the mechanically
  correct pendulum model.
- Over the physiologically reported plyometric push-up flight-time range (0.25-0.55 s), this
  translates to errors of up to 18.82% in flight-time-based estimates -- directly relevant because
  flight-time is the input several other studies in this folder (dhahbi2017, zalleg2020,
  garciamasso2011) use to back out peak force/power via free-fall kinematics.
- The overestimation grows nonlinearly and is proportionally LARGER at shorter pendulum-arm
  (i.e. shorter-limbed / more crouched) configurations: 18.18% error at t_flight = 0.30 s for
  L_OW = 0.50 m vs 10.91% at the same flight time for L_OW = 1.00 m.
- g = 9.81 m/s^2 used throughout; simulation implemented in R, adaptive Gauss-Kronrod quadrature
  (rel. tolerance 1e-10) cross-checked against ODE integration (max inter-method discrepancy
  2.5e-7 s).
