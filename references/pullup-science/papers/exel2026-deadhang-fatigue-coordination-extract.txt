Results The repeated‐measures ANOVA indicated a significant joint effect ( F = 4.1, p = 0.02, partial ηp 2 = 0.22) and significant phase*joints interaction ( F = 9.1, p &lt; 0.001, partial ηp 2 = 0.38) for r CV, whereas the main effect of phase was not significant ( F = 2.8, p = 0.10, partial ηp 2 = 0.09). Thus, as shown in Figure 3 , fatigue‐related changes in r CV were not uniform across joints, but reflected a joint‐specific redistribution pattern, primarily driven by the reduction in wrist r CV. The mean total hanging time was 52.06 ± 15.95 s. No significant difference was observed between elbow and shoulder at END after post hoc correction ( p = 0.05). The post‐hoc analysis for joints revealed that the shoulder had a larger mean r CV (328 ± 109%) compared to the elbow (342 ± 368%; p = 0.03), while the other joint comparisons were not significant (Figure 3 ). The significant phase × joint interaction showed that fatigue‐related changes in coordination variability depended on the joint. Specifically, at START, the wrist showed a larger r CV (39.6 ± 32.9%) than the elbow (9.28 ± 5.17%; p = 0.02). The wrist r CV decreased significantly from START to END (39.6 ± 32.9% at START vs. 21.2 ± 12.4% at the END; p = 0.002). Figures 4 and 5 show the mean differences for COH and MI. βCOH were significantly higher at the END for the following postural muscles pairs: brachioradialis‐biceps ( p = 0.04; Z = −2.04), trapezius‐biceps ( p = 0.04; Z = −2.04), and trapezius‐brachioradialis ( p = 0.003; Z = −1.60). Noteworthy, trapezius‐finger extensor also showed an increased βCOH at the END ( p = 0.05; Z = −1.95). The γCOH between trapezius‐brachioradialis was significantly lower at the END ( p = 0.01; Z = −2.40). βMI increased at the END for the brachioradialis with itself ( p = 0.01, t = −3.16) and with biceps ( p = 0.01; t = −2.82). γMI was higher at the END for the finger flexors with itself ( p = 0.03; t = −2.52). The repeated‐measures correlation analysis (Table 1 ) revealed significant inverse within‐subject associations between wrist r CV and trapezius–brachioradialis γCOH ( r rm = −0.57, p = 0.05), as well as between wrist r CV and finger‐flexor γMI ( r rm = −0.61, p = 0.03). Specifically, lower wrist coordination variability was associated with higher intermuscular coherence in the trapezius–brachioradialis pair and greater mutual information within the finger flexors during fatiguing dead hangs.
