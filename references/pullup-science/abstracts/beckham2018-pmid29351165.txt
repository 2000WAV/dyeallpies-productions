The Relationship between Maximum Pull-up Repetitions and First Repetition Mean Concentric Velocity.
Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 32(7), 1831-1837.

ABSTRACT:
Forty-nine participants performed a single, isolated pull-up while mean concentric velocity (MCV) was
recorded with a linear position transducer, then immediately performed a set of pull-ups to failure.
There was a statistically significant linear relationship between the MCV of the single first
repetition and the maximum number of pull-ups subsequently completed to failure (R = 0.841,
prediction error approximately 2.07 repetitions). The authors propose that measuring the velocity of
just one pull-up can estimate how many total repetitions a person could complete to failure, without
needing to actually perform a set to failure.

Notes for this project: this is the key study for "first-rep velocity predicts total reps available,"
i.e. a legitimate substitute for the pipeline's current "effort_x = fastest-rep-in-this-set-velocity /
this-rep-velocity" heuristic. Rather than normalising to the fastest rep actually observed in a
possibly-truncated set, the pipeline could normalise to the velocity expected from a person's own first
(freshest) rep using this R=0.841 regression, and then derive RIR from where the current rep's velocity
falls on that predicted total-reps curve. Caveat: 49 general trained subjects, bodyweight only, no
report of grip/sex breakdown in the abstract; prediction error of ~2 reps is not small for someone whose
total is under 8.
