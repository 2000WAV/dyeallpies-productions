ABSTRACT:
Ten male climbers (self-reported maximal climbing grade 6b-8b French scale) completed two identical
sessions. In each session they performed two blocks of two pull-ups on a large hold and on four small
climbing edges (25, 20, 15, 10 mm), order randomised. Mean propulsive velocity (MPV) was recorded with
a linear position transducer. MPV fell with hold size: large hold 0.84 +/- 0.16 m/s, 20 mm 0.75 +/- 0.16
m/s, 15 mm 0.73 +/- 0.16 m/s, 10 mm 0.52 +/- 0.15 m/s. Intraclass correlation coefficients for MPV were
good-to-excellent both within a session (ICC 0.84-0.99) and between the two sessions (ICC 0.73-0.96)
across all hold sizes. The authors propose MPV during small-edge pull-ups as a sport-specific
finger/elbow/shoulder power test for climbers.

Notes for this project: this is the closest published pull-up-specific reliability figure for MPV
measured with an instrumented device (linear position transducer, not smartphone video) — ICC 0.73-0.96
between sessions is the benchmark to compare the pipeline's pose-based velocity reliability against.
Also gives a real MPV range (0.52-0.84 m/s) for bodyweight pull-ups under different grip/friction
conditions, useful context for what counts as a "fast" vs a "grip-limited" rep.
