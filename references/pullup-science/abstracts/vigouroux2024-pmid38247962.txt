ABSTRACT:
Thirty experienced climbers were assigned to eccentric, isometric, plyometric or control training
protocols over 5 weeks of pull-up-focused training. All three active intervention groups improved
maximum strength. Velocity variables (peak/mean pull velocity) improved specifically in the
eccentric (ECC) and plyometric (PLYO) groups; total muscle work performed during a max-rep set
increased specifically in the PLYO group (+21.9 +/- 16.6%, p = 0.015). The authors conclude the
contraction regimen used in training selectively shapes which mechanical outputs (force, velocity,
work capacity) improve, i.e. velocity gains and endurance/work-capacity gains are somewhat separable
training effects rather than one generic "getting stronger."

Notes for this project: relevant to the "effort/RIR" replacement because it shows pull velocity and
total work-to-failure can move independently depending on training history — a fixed velocity-loss-per-
rep constant will not generalise across people with different training backgrounds (plyometric-trained
people can sustain more total work at a given velocity loss). Should be listed as a caveat on any new
formula, not baked into the model.
