ABSTRACT:
Twenty-eight climbers of varying levels performed countermovement jump tests, progressively weighted
pull-ups, and pull-up sets to failure. Muscle work performed during the max-rep set and the maximum
number of pull-ups both correlated with climbing performance level; the authors identify fatigue
resistance (capacity to keep producing force/work over repeated reps) as a critical determinant of
climbing-relevant pull-up capability, separate from peak force or peak velocity, which were more
individual-specific and less predictive of climbing level on their own.

Notes for this project: supports reporting a work-capacity / fatigue-resistance metric (e.g. total
mechanical work sustained across the set relative to early-set work) as a distinct output from peak
power or peak velocity, rather than collapsing everything into a single "effort" multiplier.
