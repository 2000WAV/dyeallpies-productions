ABSTRACT:
Systematic review + meta-analysis/meta-regression (PRISMA) of 18 acute and 19 longitudinal studies using
velocity loss (VL) to prescribe or monitor resistance training. As VL within a set increases: reps per
set, blood lactate and RPE generally increase, while countermovement-jump height, sprint time and
velocity against a fixed submaximal load generally decrease — but the size of these effects depends on
exercise and load used. Chronically, VL threshold did not affect strength or muscular-endurance gains;
higher VL thresholds favoured hypertrophy (meta-regression b = 0.006, 95% CI 0.001-0.012 per unit VL);
lower VL thresholds favoured countermovement-jump (b = -0.040, CI -0.079 to -0.001), sprint (b = 0.001,
CI 0.001-0.002) and submaximal-velocity performance (b = -0.018, CI -0.029 to -0.006) outcomes.
CONCLUSION: VL is a real graded dose-response variable for acute fatigue and volume, but its chronic
training effect is adaptation-specific (hypertrophy vs. power/speed) and moderated by exercise/load/
individual factors, not a single universal "stop here" number.

Notes for this project: broad VBT context for why "one velocity-loss constant" is an oversimplification
in general, reinforcing the pull-up-specific finding in Sánchez-Moreno 2020 (25% vs 50% VL) — i.e. VL25
is specifically the strength/velocity-adaptation-optimal stopping point for this exercise, matching this
review's general finding that lower VL thresholds favour velocity/power-type outcomes over hypertrophy.
