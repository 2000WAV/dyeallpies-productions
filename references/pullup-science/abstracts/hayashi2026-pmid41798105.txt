Relationship Between Mechanical Variables and Maximum Strength in Countermovement and Pure Concentric

ABSTRACT:
Fifty male collegiate athletes performed countermovement pull-ups (CPU, a slight controlled downward
counter-movement from a dead hang before pulling, using the stretch-shortening cycle) and pure
concentric pull-ups (PCP, starting from a static dead hang with no counter-movement) while mean/peak
velocity, power, and an isometric mid-hang pull-up strength test (LP-IMS) were recorded. Vmean and Vmax
of PCP reps showed significant strong positive correlations with LP-IMS strength; the corresponding
correlations for CPU reps were weaker, which the authors attribute to stretch-shortening-cycle (SSC)
contribution in the countermovement variant partially decoupling bar velocity from underlying maximal
strength.

Notes for this project: directly relevant to the pipeline's velocity-based effort/RIR estimate, because
it shows the velocity-to-strength (and by extension velocity-to-effort) relationship is tighter for a
strict dead-hang start than for any rep style with a counter-movement/kip component. Any velocity-based
RIR or %1RM estimate should specify or detect whether the rep started from a true dead hang (pure
concentric) vs. a bounced/countermovement start, since the same velocity means different things in the
two cases.
