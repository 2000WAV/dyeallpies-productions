Alterations in kinematics and muscle activation patterns with the addition of a 
kipping action during a pull-up activity.

Dinunzio C(1), Porter N(1), Van Scoy J(1), Cordice D(1), McCulloch RS(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Human Physiology, School of Nursing and Human Physiology, 
Gonzaga University , Spokane , WA , USA.

Recently, addition of a gymnastics glide kip to a standard pull-up (SPU) has 
resulted in the kipping pull-up (KPU). Changes in muscle activation and 
kinematics were evaluated with eleven athletes performing sets of 5 SPU and 5 
KPU. Surface electromyography of upper body and lower body muscles was recorded 
along with movement kinematics obtained via markers and motion tracking 
software. Most kinematic variables were significantly higher in the KPU 
including (KPU minus SPU deg): Max hip angle (48.8° ± 6.8°, p < 0.001) and max 
knee angle (56.5° ± 11.3°, p < 0.001). The recruitment of core and lower body 
muscles was significantly higher in the KPU (% MVIC increase): rectus abdominis 
(28.7 ± 4.7%, p < 0.001), external oblique (21.8 ± 4.1%, p < 0.001), iliopsoas 
(26.1 ± 5.5%, p = 0.001) and tensor fasciae latae (13.5 ± 2.3%, p < 0.001). 
Correspondingly, the biceps brachii had lower activation in the KPU (% MVIC 
decrease): (26.7 ± 0.6%, p = 0.006). Depending on the athlete's goal, they may 
elect to perform an SPU for higher upper body muscle activation; or the KPU for 
more full-body activation with the potential to perform more repetitions through 
reduced upper body fatigue.
