ABSTRACT (as returned by PubMed / reconstructed from indexed record):
Fifty-two men performed a pull-up 1RM test (own body mass plus added load where required) and a set
to failure at several relative loads. Mean propulsive velocity (MPV) was recorded on every repetition
with a linear velocity transducer. A strong relationship was found between MPV and %1RM (r = -.96).
Mean velocity attained at 1RM load (V1RM) was 0.20 +/- 0.05 m/s, and this V1RM value influenced the
MPV that corresponded to each %1RM for a given individual. A strong relationship was also found
between the percentage of velocity loss within a set and the percentage of the maximum possible
repetitions already completed (R^2 = .88); this relationship remained essentially unchanged after a
12-week training program that increased maximal strength and the maximum number of repetitions by
about 15%. The authors conclude that monitoring repetition velocity in the pull-up allows: (1)
estimation of the %1RM being lifted from the velocity of the first repetition performed with a given
load, and (2) estimation of the number of repetitions left in reserve from the amount of velocity loss
already accrued within the set, without needing to know the individual's true 1RM in advance.

Notes for this project: this is the primary source for the "velocity loss % <-> reps-already-done %"
relationship used to replace the pipeline's flat 9%-per-rep RIR heuristic. V1RM ~0.20 m/s is a body-mass
prone/pronated-grip value from trained but not elite men; individual V1RM varies +/-0.05 m/s (25% CV),
so an absolute-velocity RIR estimate needs a personal calibration rep, not a fixed universal cutoff.
