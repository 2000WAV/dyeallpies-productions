Fetched from PubMed E-utilities on 2026-09-08.

ABSTRACT:
Ten elite powerlifters performed reps at 50-100% 1RM across bench press, back squat and hip thrust,
while a linear position transducer (reference), two wearable inertial devices (wrist-worn and
barbell-mounted "Beast"), and a smartphone camera app (PowerLift, iOS) all recorded bar velocity
simultaneously. All device types correlated highly with the transducer (r = 0.94-0.98 depending on
device/exercise, standard error of the estimate 0.03-0.07 m/s); reliability (ICC) ranged 0.910-0.990
across devices and exercises. The authors conclude both wearable and smartphone-camera-based systems are
valid, reliable alternatives to a linear transducer for measuring barbell velocity in common barbell
exercises.

Notes for this project: not pull-up-specific and not pose-tracking (a fixed camera tracking a bar
marker, not a person), but the closest available validity/reliability numbers for camera-based velocity
measurement in resistance exercise generally (r >= 0.94, ICC >= 0.91). Cited here as the best available
proxy for "how much to trust a video-derived velocity number," while flagging that markerless full-body
pose tracking of a moving person (as this pipeline does) has different, generally worse, expected error
sources (self-occlusion, landmark jitter at the wrist/shoulder, camera-plane parallax) than a barbell
marker tracked by a purpose-built app — no pull-up-specific or markerless-pose-specific validity study
was found in this search. ESTIMATE: treat the pipeline's own velocity numbers as directionally correct
rep-to-rep but not validated to transducer-level absolute accuracy.
