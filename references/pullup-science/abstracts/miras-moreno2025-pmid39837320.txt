Improving the Use of Lifting Velocity to Predict Repetitions to Failure: A Systematic Review.
International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance, 20(3), 335-344.
PMID 39837320. Closed access (Human Kinetics; no PMC copy found).

Fetched from PubMed E-utilities on 2026-09-08.

ABSTRACT:
BACKGROUND: velocity-based training can estimate the maximum number of repetitions to failure (RTF)
from the fastest velocity recorded within a set, but no prior review had examined the fundamental
properties of the RTF-velocity relationship. PURPOSE: (1) determine goodness of fit, reliability and
accuracy of RTF-velocity relationships; (2) give guidance on methodological factors affecting accuracy.
METHODS: PubMed, SPORTDiscus and Scopus were searched for studies with >=2 sets to failure at different
loads, multijoint exercises, RTF and fastest velocity recorded per set. RESULTS: six qualifying studies
showed (1) robust goodness of fit, (2) acceptable-to-high between-session reliability of the velocity
associated with each RTF value (1-15 reps to failure), and (3) acceptable RTF-prediction accuracy in
fatigue-free conditions (long inter-set rest), but accuracy was compromised under fatigue (short
inter-set rest) except in athletes with more than 2 years of training-to-failure experience.
CONCLUSIONS: relationship properties hold regardless of exercise (upper vs. lower body), equipment
(Smith machine vs. free weight), velocity variable (mean vs. peak), or rest duration (5-10 min); but the
regression approach (multiple-point vs. two-point) changes accuracy, and training-to-failure experience
is critical to accuracy.

Notes for this project: general (non-pull-up-specific) systematic review supporting velocity-based RTF
prediction as a class of method, with the key caveat that inter-set fatigue and lack of training-to-
failure experience both degrade prediction accuracy — relevant since the pipeline's video is typically a
single set (no inter-set fatigue issue) but the subject's training-to-failure experience is unknown, so
any RTF/RIR estimate should be flagged with an uncertainty band rather than reported as exact.
