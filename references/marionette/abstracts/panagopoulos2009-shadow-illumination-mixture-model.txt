Panagopoulos, A., Wang, C., Samaras, D., and Paragios, N. (2009). Robust Shadow and
Illumination Estimation Using a Mixture Model. CVPR 2009.

STATUS: full text saved (Stony Brook CVL mirror), text-extracted; see
papers/panagopoulos2009-shadow-illumination-mixture-model.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: Proposes a graphical model that jointly estimates cast shadows and the
scene's illumination environment from a SINGLE image with only coarse 3D geometry
(no known fine occluder shape, unlike Sato et al.). Illumination is represented as
a mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions on the sphere of directions (a
smooth, multi-lobe approximation to real skylight + sun / window light). A Markov
Random Field ties per-pixel shadow evidence (from image intensity/texture) to the
illumination model, so the two estimates regularize each other; solved with
graph-cut / EM-style alternation.

RELEVANCE: shows how to go from a single ordinary photo (no calibration target,
no known occluder) to an illumination *direction + spread* estimate using shadow
evidence alone -- the same category of problem as inferring the window light's
direction and angular size from the hand's soft shadow on the wall, but at a
level of machinery (MRF + mixture model) beyond what this small compositing task
needs. Cited in 05-camera-light-compositing.md as the "single-image, unknown
occluder" branch of the shadow-to-light literature, contrasted with the simpler
penumbra-geometry approach actually used.
