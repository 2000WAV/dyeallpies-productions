Rademacher, P., Lengyel, J., Cutrell, E., and Whitted, T. (2001). Measuring
the Perception of Visual Realism in Images. Proceedings of the 12th
Eurographics Workshop on Rendering Techniques (EGWR 2001), 235-247.

STATUS: full text saved (author-hosted mirror via Edward Cutrell's site);
see papers/rademacher2001-visual-realism.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: forced-choice ("real"/"not real") psychophysics experiments on
photographs and matched CG renderings, varying one visual factor at a time:
shadow softness (penumbra angle), surface smoothness, number of light
sources, number of objects, and variety of object shapes. Uses a repeated-
measures logistic regression on the binary response.

KEY NUMBERS:
- Shadow softness: five penumbra levels measured at 0.39, 1.5, 2.5, 5.2 and
  10.3 degrees. Realism rating became statistically significant starting at
  the 5.21-degree level (chi^2=5.39, df=1, p=.0203) versus the sharpest
  (0.39-degree) shadows; no significant difference between the two softest
  levels (10.3 vs 5.2 degrees; chi^2=2.64, p=.1043). I.e. softening a shadow
  buys realism only up to roughly 5 degrees of penumbra angle -- beyond that,
  diminishing returns.
- Surface smoothness: smooth (spray-painted) blocks rated tau=.39 "real";
  rough (brush-painted, visible brush marks) blocks rated tau=.71 "real" --
  a large, statistically strong effect in the OPPOSITE direction from a
  naive "smoother = more realistic" assumption. Perfectly smooth CG surfaces
  read as less real than surfaces with visible texture/imperfection.

RELEVANCE: directly informs items 3 (shadow softness on the wall) and 4
(materials) of 11-cg-realism-in-phone-footage.md -- a ~5-degree penumbra is
the target softness for the marionette's own cast shadow if one is added,
and a small amount of surface roughness/imperfection on the puppet's paint
(vs. a perfectly smooth solid-red render) is evidence-backed, not just a
stylistic guess.
