Yamane, K., Hodgins, J.K., and Brown, H.B. (2003). Controlling a marionette with
human motion capture data. Proceedings of the 2003 IEEE International Conference
on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), pp. 3834-3841.

ABSTRACT (as printed):
In this paper, we present a method for controlling a motorized, string-driven
marionette using motion capture data from human actors. The motion data must be
adapted for the marionette because its kinematic and dynamic properties differ
from those of the human actor in degrees of freedom, limb length, workspace, mass
distribution, sensors, and actuators. This adaptation is accomplished via an inverse
kinematics algorithm that takes into account marker positions, joint motion ranges,
string constraints, and potential energy. We also apply a feedforward controller to
prevent extraneous swings of the hands. Experimental results show that our approach
enables the marionette to perform motions that are qualitatively similar to the
original human motion capture data.

KEY NUMBERS / DETAILS PULLED FROM THE FULL TEXT (papers/yamane2003-marionette-icra.pdf
/.txt):
- The physical marionette used is about 60 cm tall (roughly twice the ~30 cm figure
  planned for this project).
- The shoulder and elbow joints have cloth stops to prevent unrealistic joint angles --
  i.e. the joint's range of motion is limited passively by a soft cloth stop, not a rigid
  mechanical hinge stop. This is the closest primary-source number this search found for
  "how limp is a marionette joint": the joint is otherwise free/underdamped, and only a
  soft cloth tether stops over-rotation.
- Swing dynamics of the hands (after a string is released/slackened) are modeled as a
  pendulum with a moving base; the linearized equation of motion is of the form
  y'' = -(g/l)(x - y) - d y', with l the pendulum length and d a damping term fit
  experimentally per joint (exact fitted value of d not given in the extracted text --
  NOT FOUND as a number, only as a fitted-per-joint free parameter of their model).
- The dynamic model treats each string as a constraint that goes slack whenever the
  required tension would be negative (string can only pull), consistent with the
  unilateral-tendon description also used by Johnson & Murphey (2007, see
  johnson2007-dynamic-modeling-marionettes.txt) and adopted for MuJoCo's own
  "unactuated tendon with range [0 X]" cable behaviour.
