Debevec, P. (1998). Rendering Synthetic Objects into Real Scenes: Bridging
Traditional and Image-Based Graphics with Global Illumination and High
Dynamic Range Photography. SIGGRAPH 1998, 189-198.

STATUS: full text saved (author-hosted, pauldebevec.com); see
papers/debevec1998-rendering-synthetic-objects.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: the founding paper for image-based lighting and the "shadow
catcher" / differential rendering technique used throughout modern VFX
(and already referenced qualitatively in this project's item 05, section
2.3). Splits a scene into three parts -- the DISTANT scene (captured as an
HDR light probe, providing incident illumination), the LOCAL scene (the
real surfaces nearest the inserted object, whose reflectance is estimated
approximately), and the SYNTHETIC OBJECT itself. DIFFERENTIAL RENDERING:
render the local scene twice with a global-illumination solver, once with
the synthetic object present and once without; the difference between the
two renders is exactly the object's lighting contribution (new shadows,
reflected/bounced light) to the local scene, which can then be added onto
the real photograph without needing a fully accurate BRDF for the real
surfaces -- only their APPROXIMATE reflectance, since errors mostly cancel
in the differencing.

KEY NUMBERS: none of the form "X% error" -- this is a rendering technique
paper, not a perceptual study; its contribution is the differential-
rendering formula/workflow itself (object-present render minus object-
absent render, added onto the plate), already summarized in item 05's
shadow-catcher discussion.

RELEVANCE: item 11 section 3's citation for WHY the shadow-catcher approach
(already flagged as a "nice-to-have" in item 05 for this shot) is the
standard, correct way to add a plausible contact/cast shadow for the
marionette onto the real wall without needing to model the wall's exact
paint BRDF -- only an approximate one, since differential rendering cancels
most of that error.
