SUMMARY: biographical/critical entry on Jiri Trnka (1912-1969), the Czech
puppet-film director/sculptor whose stop-motion marionette animation
(founded his studio in 1946) conveyed character and drama primarily through
BODY LANGUAGE, staging, expressive lighting and camera movement rather than
facial animation -- his puppets typically have simplified, near-expressionless
carved faces, and the ILLUSION OF LIFE is built from posture, timing and
lighting, not from detailed facial realism.

KEY NUMBERS: none (biographical/critical text, not a quantitative source).

RELEVANCE: item 11 section 6 -- a strong precedent for THIS project's own
figure: a marionette with a simplified, non-detailed face/body (exactly
what the current capsule/ellipsoid MuJoCo figure is) can still read as
alive and expressive if the STAGING (pose, motion, lighting direction) does
the work, consistent with Kleffner & Ramachandran 1992's shape-from-shading
argument (item 09) that one consistent overhead-ish key light reads as
"solid" almost pre-attentively, independent of surface detail.
