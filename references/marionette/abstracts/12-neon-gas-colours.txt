Real neon-sign gas colours (Neon lighting article, "History and science" section):
- Neon gas itself: the "popular orange" glow neon signs are named for (characteristic
  orange-red).
- Mercury (vapour): blue. Mixing metallic mercury with neon gas also creates blue.
- Hydrogen: purple-red.
- Helium: yellow or pink.
- Carbon dioxide: white.
- Green is achieved indirectly, by passing a mercury/argon tube's light through
  uranium (yellow) glass, not by a single gas's own emission.
- White and gold: argon + helium.
- Fluorescent-coated tubes (argon/mercury-vapour mixture emitting UV that excites a
  phosphor coating) extend the palette to "nearly 100" additional colours since the
  1950s (television-phosphor-derived coatings) -- this is the mechanism behind
  magenta/cyan/other non-primary neon-sign colours, which are phosphor conversions of
  UV, not raw noble-gas glow.
- Tube outer diameter 9-25 mm; fill pressure 3-20 Torr (0.4-3 kPa).

Violet specifically (Argon article, infobox + main text): "Gas-discharge lamps filled
with PURE ARGON provide lilac/violet light; with argon and some mercury, blue." The
infobox image caption independently describes a vial of argon showing "a violet glow
when placed in an electric field." This is the direct real-world "classic neon gas
colour" anchor for a dominant-violet neon figure: pure argon glow IS the real-world
gas-discharge violet, sitting alongside neon's orange-red, mercury's blue, hydrogen's
purple-red and helium's yellow/pink as the small set of true single-gas neon colours;
magenta and cyan are phosphor conversions rather than raw gas colours.
