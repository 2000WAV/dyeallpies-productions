SUMMARY: three psychophysical experiments measuring how accurately subjects
match the position, orientation and size of a "test" object to a "standard"
by interactively manipulating it, under different combinations of pictorial
depth cues (shadows, motion parallax, texture, perspective). Not all cues
matter equally: texture had only a small effect, shadows had a MAJOR
effect on relative-position judgments, and motion parallax's effect on
performance was inconsistent/task-dependent (sometimes it helped, sometimes
it did not, depending on what was being judged). This is one of the
founding studies (with Kersten et al. 1997) establishing that cast shadows
are an outsized depth/spatial cue relative to their rendering cost.
