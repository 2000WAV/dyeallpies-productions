Maggioni, V., Azevedo-Coste, C., Durand, S., & Bailly, F. (2025). Optimisation and Comparison
of Markerless and Marker-Based Motion Capture Methods for Hand and Finger Movement Analysis.
Sensors, 25(4), 1079. PMID 40006308, PMCID PMC11858933. Open access (MDPI, CC BY).

ABSTRACT (as printed; full article text saved in
papers/maggioni2025-markerless-vs-markerbased-hand-mocap.html and the plain-text extract
papers/maggioni2025-markerless-vs-markerbased-hand-mocap.txt -- the PMC PDF endpoint
returned a bot-check page rather than a PDF, so the archived copy is the article's own HTML,
which carries the full text under a CC BY licence):

Ensuring the accurate tracking of hand and fingers movements is an ongoing challenge for
upper limb rehabilitation assessment, as the high number of degrees of freedom and segments
in the limited volume of the hand makes this a difficult task. The objective of this study is
to evaluate the performance of two markerless approaches (the Leap Motion Controller and the
Google MediaPipe API) in comparison to a marker-based one, and to improve the precision of the
markerless methods by introducing additional data processing algorithms fusing multiple
recording devices. Fifteen healthy participants were instructed to perform five distinct hand
movements while being recorded by the three motion capture methods simultaneously. The
captured movement data from each device was analyzed using a skeletal model of the hand
through the inverse kinematics method of the OpenSim software. Finally, the root mean square
errors of the angles formed by each finger segment were calculated for the markerless and
marker-based motion capture methods to compare their accuracy. Our results indicate that the
MediaPipe-based setup is more accurate than the Leap Motion Controller-based one (average
root mean square error of 10.9 deg versus 14.7 deg), showing promising results for the use of
markerless-based methods in clinical applications.

RELEVANCE TO THIS PROJECT: the only independent, quantitative accuracy check found for
MediaPipe Hands against a marker-based (Optitrack) ground truth, covering exactly the failure
modes the task brief asks about. Key numbers, all for the "MP" (MediaPipe, v0.10.15, 30 Hz,
4-camera) condition, averaged over 15 participants: mean joint-angle RMSE 10.9 +/- 7.8 deg
across all tasks (vs. 14.7 +/- 8.6 deg for Leap Motion); per-joint RMSE mostly 8-11 deg
except the thumb MP joint ("1mp"), 27.3 +/- 8.1 deg (the paper's largest single-joint error).
The paper's own translation of these angle errors: "considering the length of a finger
segment between two joints, this range of error (10 deg to 15 deg) is equivalent to a distal
spatial error of a few (around two to three) millimeters" -- i.e. fingertip-level spatial
error is on the order of 2-3 mm even under good conditions (static, unoccluded palm-down
poses). Separately and directly on this project's two named failure modes: (1) on the
relative-depth (z) channel -- "preliminary testing revealed that this third depth coordinate
was unreliable, frequently causing the estimated hand to adopt impossible poses" (Section
2.4) -- corroborates the build's choice to hold depth constant and solve pose by PnP rather
than trust MediaPipe's own z; (2) on occlusion/closed-fist-like poses -- "these initial tests
also showed that the landmarks of occluded fingers were not concurrent with the actual
position of said fingers" and the "flexion" task (full finger flexion, fingertips hidden
under the palm -- the closest analog in this study to a closed fist) is flagged as the task
"evaluating their robustness to a high level of self-occlusion when the fingertips are
completely hidden beneath the palm" (Section 2.3), with marker/landmark occlusion rates
reaching 16.6 +/- 18.8% of frames in that task. No dorsal (back-of-hand) viewpoint was tested
in this study -- all recordings were "palm facing down (towards the LMC)" -- so it does not
directly speak to the back-of-hand camera angle used in this project's shot; that specific
gap is marked NOT FOUND in the main writeup.
