Sato, I., Sato, Y., and Ikeuchi, K. (2003). Illumination from Shadows.
IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, 25(3), 290-300.
(Earlier conference version: Sato, Sato & Ikeuchi, "Illumination Distribution from
Shadows," CVPR 1999.)

STATUS: full text saved (author mirror, NII), text-extracted; see
papers/sato2003-illumination-from-shadows.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: Recovers a scene's illumination distribution (as a set of discrete point
sources on a hemisphere) from image brightness measured INSIDE the shadow cast by
an object of known 3D shape. Key idea: a shadow region is occluded from some
directions of the incident illumination and not others; by knowing the object's
shape (hence exactly which sky/light directions are blocked at each shadow pixel)
and measuring the residual brightness in the shadow (indirect/ambient light plus
any partially-visible sources), a linear system in the unknown per-direction
radiances can be solved. Handles unknown surface reflectance jointly, and uses an
adaptive (coarse-to-fine) sampling of light directions instead of a dense fixed
grid. Demonstrated on real photographs with a calibrated occluder of known
geometry casting a shadow on a diffuse surface.

RELEVANCE: the theoretical basis for "read the light from the shadow it casts."
For the marionette shot: the real hand occludes the window light and casts a soft
shadow on the wall; the same occlusion logic (in reverse -- reasoning from
penumbra width/shape back to source position + angular size) is the basis of the
light-and-shadow estimate in 05-camera-light-compositing.md. This paper assumes a
known occluder shape and solves for the light; our pipeline instead uses the
simpler classical penumbra-geometry relation (see Fernando 2005 / real-time
soft-shadow literature) because the hand's shape is already known from the shot
setup and we only need light DIRECTION + ANGULAR SIZE, not a full incident-light
hemisphere.
