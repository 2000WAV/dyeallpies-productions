Lee, K-R. and Ju, Y-G. (2020). Measurement of resistance coefficients of pendulum
motion with balls of various sizes. arXiv:2002.03796.
(Filename kept as salcedo2020 for continuity with the download log; actual authors
are Lee and Ju, Kyungpook National University -- corrected here.)

ABSTRACT (as printed):
In order to obtain the damping and resistance coefficients of a pendulum, we
constructed an optical system containing a photogate for measuring the speed of
the pendulum at the lowest point of motion. The photogate consisted of a
photoresistor, a laser, a mechanical body, and a pendulum ball. A 3D printer was
used to produce the mechanical body and pendulum balls of various sizes.
Furthermore, we used Arduino to automate measurement of the speed at the lowest
point of motion and increase the precision. We found that the resistance
coefficient was proportional to the size of the balls, regardless of the ball mass,
in agreement with the drag equation for a small Reynolds number.

KEY NUMBERS (Table I / results section, full text in
papers/salcedo2020-pendulum-resistance-coefficients.pdf/.txt): for pendulum balls
of diameter 11.5, 18.7, 24.1, 30.0, and 36.0 mm -- a size range that brackets the
hand/head/foot segments of a ~30 cm marionette figure -- the measured exponential
DAMPING COEFFICIENT (gamma in x(t) = A e^{-gamma t} cos(omega t), units 1/s) was
0.0096, 0.0086, 0.0070, 0.0067, and 0.0056 s^-1 respectively, i.e. DECREASING with
increasing ball size/mass even though the drag force itself increases with size
(because mass grows faster than drag as the ball gets bigger). The corresponding
resistance coefficients c (in the linear-drag model F = -c*v, units kg/s) were
1.2e-4, 1.4e-4, 1.8e-4, 2.2e-4, and 2.6e-4 kg/s, confirming c is proportional to
ball radius (Stokes-like drag regime for these low speeds/sizes).

RELEVANCE: gives real, measured order-of-magnitude damping coefficients for
small pendulum bobs in air, appropriate for estimating how much a ~30 cm
marionette's free swing decays per period from air drag alone (aside from any
extra damping added at the joints/strings). At gamma ~ 0.006-0.01 /s and a typical
period of order 1 s for a limb-scale pendulum, air drag alone gives a logarithmic
decrement of only ~0.006-0.01 per swing -- i.e. air drag is NOT the main source of
settling in a 7 s marionette shot; joint/string damping must do most of the work,
consistent with Yamane et al. (2003) needing a fitted damping term at the string
attachment rather than relying on air drag.
