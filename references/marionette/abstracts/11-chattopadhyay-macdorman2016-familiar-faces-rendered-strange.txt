Chattopadhyay, D., and MacDorman, K.F. (2016). Familiar Faces Rendered
Strange: Why Inconsistent Realism Drives Characters into the Uncanny
Valley. Journal of Vision 16(11):7, 1-25. DOI: 10.1167/16.11.7.

STATUS: full text saved (MacDorman's own site); see
papers/chattopadhyay-macdorman2016-familiar-faces-rendered-strange.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: 365 participants categorized and rated objects, animals and humans
whose realism was manipulated along two kinds of transitions: one that
REDUCED realism CONSISTENCY across the image/features (some parts more
realistic than others) and a matched control that reduced realism
uniformly. Key result: reducing realism consistency did NOT make objects in
general appear less familiar, but for animals and humans specifically it
did, and specifically drove COLD, EERIE feelings (the uncanny-valley
affect) -- i.e. the uncanny valley is better explained by INCONSISTENT
realism across a humanlike figure than by low realism per se. A uniformly
low-realism (fully stylized) character avoids the effect; a character that
mixes realism levels across its features (a realistic face on a cartoonish
body, or vice versa) is the actual trigger.

KEY NUMBERS: n=365 participants; realism inconsistency measured
objectively via a two-dimensional Morlet wavelet decomposition (a spatial-
frequency-domain measure, not a simple percentage) rather than a single
scalar -- no single "% inconsistency threshold" number is quotable from the
abstract/intro; the qualitative finding (inconsistency, not low realism
itself, drives eeriness) is the load-bearing result for this project.

RELEVANCE: the single most directly load-bearing uncanny-valley citation
for this project's ranked checklist (section 7): the marionette's smooth
capsules/ellipsoids, single solid-red material, and uniform shading style
should stay CONSISTENT across every body part -- adding, say, a highly
detailed, differently-shaded face or hands while the rest of the body stays
primitive-shaped would risk the exact inconsistency this paper shows
triggers uncanny eeriness, worse than just leaving the whole figure
uniformly stylized/low-realism as it is now.
