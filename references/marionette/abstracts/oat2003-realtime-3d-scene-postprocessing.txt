Oat, C. (2003). Real-Time 3D Scene Post-Processing. Game Developers Conference
Europe 2003 (ATI Research).

STATUS: full text saved (author's own site), text-extracted; see
papers/oat2003-realtime-3d-scene-postprocessing.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: A survey and tutorial covering HDR rendering, bloom, tone mapping,
vignette, depth of field and heat-haze as real-time post effects, framed
explicitly as mimicking the physical properties of real-world illumination and
the imaging devices used to capture it. Documents Kawase's bloom filter (Masaki
Kawase, GDC 2003, DOUBLE-S.T.E.A.L.) in detail, including the actual downsample
kernel weights used at each iteration (a 3x3 kernel with weights 1/16, 2/16,
4/16 arranged binomially) and how iterating the filter at progressively coarser
downsample levels builds a wide, cheap approximate Gaussian bloom.

RELEVANCE: primary contemporary source, alongside the original Kawase GDC talk,
for the exact bloom kernel weights and the downsample-iterate-upsample bloom
recipe used for the marionette's glow. Also frames bloom explicitly as camera or
film sensor bleed, i.e. an optical or sensor artifact worth reproducing so the
neon object reads as filmed by the same phone camera as the plate, rather than
composited flat on top of it.
