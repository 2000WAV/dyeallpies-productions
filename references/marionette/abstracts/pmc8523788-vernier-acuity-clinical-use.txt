SUMMARY / KEY NUMBERS (for the string-visibility calc):
- Standard resolution acuity (the ordinary "sharpness" limit, e.g. 20/20):
  minimum angle of resolution = 1 ARCMINUTE of visual angle (stated
  equivalently as 2 mm resolvable at 6 m). This is the threshold for
  resolving two separate points/lines as distinct.
- Vernier acuity / hyperacuity (detecting a small MISALIGNMENT or the mere
  presence of a fine line/edge, not resolving two separate points): as low
  as 2-5 ARCSECONDS in normal observers (untrained observers still reach
  ~10 arcsec) -- roughly 12-30x finer than the 1-arcmin resolution limit,
  and finer than the ~30-arcsec centre-to-centre spacing of foveal cone
  photoreceptors. This "hyperacuity" is explained by cortical pooling
  across many photoreceptors/orientation-tuned V1 cells, not by the raw
  optics of the eye.
RELEVANCE to item 9 (string visibility): a taut string is a LINE-detection
target, not a two-point-resolution target, so its effective visibility
threshold is much closer to the ~2-30 arcsec hyperacuity regime than to the
1-arcmin resolution regime -- i.e., a thin high-contrast line can be seen far
below the angular width that a "resolution acuity" number alone would
suggest. See data/09-colour-numbers.csv for the resulting arcsec-per-pixel
conversion at a typical phone viewing distance.
