SUMMARY: production account of LAIKA's stop-motion puppet fabrication for
Kubo and the Two Strings (2016). Combines traditional puppet-making
materials (latex, polyurethane skins over armatures) with large-scale 3D
printing (the Moonbeast character: 850 individually 3D-printed exterior
pieces over a 250-part internal armature; over 23,000 3D-printed
interchangeable face plates used across the production for replacement
facial animation) and hand craft for non-rigid elements (individual
feathers laser-cut and hand-woven with piano wire for one character;
crumpled/uncrumpled Tyvek sheets for a flexible cloth-like surface).

KEY NUMBERS: 850 printed pieces (Moonbeast exterior) + 250-part armature;
23,000+ printed face plates (whole production).

RELEVANCE: item 11 section 6 -- reinforces the same replacement-animation
principle as the Pinocchio example (discrete, purpose-built parts per
pose/expression beat convincing continuous deformation for a rigid-bodied
character), and shows that large casts of interchangeable RIGID parts (like
this project's own rigid MuJoCo capsule/ellipsoid segments) is itself an
established, successful way to build a convincing puppet, not a
compromise forced by the simulation.
