SUMMARY (from the paper's own text and its Figure 5 / boundary-ratio table):
Proportionality constants ("boundary ratios") relate individual body segment
landmark heights and lengths to total stature, following the method introduced by
Drillis and Contini (1966). The paper's Figure 5 lists, for females/males/averaged,
the 5th/50th/95th-percentile ratio of each landmark to stature, derived from the
ANSUR anthropometric survey. The 50th-percentile "averaged" values used in this
project's data/02-segment-parameters.csv (length-fraction rows) are: acromial
(shoulder) height 0.820 x stature; trochanterion (hip) height 0.528; lateral femoral
epicondyle (knee) height 0.284; lateral malleolus (ankle) height 0.038; hand length
0.111; radiale-stylion (forearm) length 0.151; acromion-radiale (upper arm) length
0.193. Thigh length and shank length are not given directly and were computed in
this project as the difference of two landmark heights (trochanterion - knee, and
knee - ankle respectively) -- see notes column in the CSV.

RELEVANCE: cross-check and fill-in for segment length fractions not tabulated
directly by de Leva (1996), which reports absolute lengths in mm for a single
reference stature rather than a general fraction-of-stature table.
