Bakhshi, S., Shamma, D.A., Kennedy, L., and Gilbert, E. (2015). Why We Filter
Our Photos and How It Impacts Engagement. Proceedings of the Ninth
International AAAI Conference on Web and Social Media (ICWSM 2015).

STATUS: full text saved (open PDF, author-hosted mirror); see
papers/bakhshi2015-why-we-filter-our-photos.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: large-scale (7.6 million Flickr photos) plus interview study of
photo-filter use and its effect on viewer engagement (views and comments).
Filtered photos overall were 21% more likely to be viewed and 45% more
likely to be commented on than unfiltered ones. Breaking this down by what a
filter actually changes: "filters that increase warmth, exposure and
contrast boost engagement the most" (verbatim, from the abstract/results).
Warmth here means shifting the colour balance warmer (more red/yellow, less
blue) -- convergent with the red/warm-hue "advantage" literature (Elliot &
Maier 2014) and with the item-9 brief's question of which hue reads best
against a WARM off-white wall (#F2F0EA is itself warm-leaning: R=242 > G=240
> B=234).

KEY NUMBERS:
- +21% relative likelihood of being viewed, filtered vs unfiltered photos.
- +45% relative likelihood of being commented on, filtered vs unfiltered
  photos.
- The three specific edits most associated with higher engagement, named in
  the paper: increased warmth, increased exposure, increased contrast.
NOTE: this is a photo-filter study, not a controlled hue/saturation
psychophysics experiment -- it supports "warm, high-contrast, saturated
imagery gets more engagement on a photo-sharing platform" as a real-world/
ecological finding, not a laboratory causal isolation of hue.
