SUMMARY: production account of Team America: World Police (2004, Trey Parker
/ Matt Stone), shot entirely with practical marionettes -- no CGI puppets.
Confirms the strings were left genuinely visible on screen throughout the
film (an aesthetic/comedic choice, not a compositing failure); the crew
deliberately timed shots so the wires would not visibly upstage whatever
joke was the focus of a given scene, rather than trying to remove or hide
them. A crew of roughly 200 people supported the shoot, with as many as
four puppeteers operating a single marionette at once for complex actions.

KEY NUMBERS: ~200-person crew; up to 4 puppeteers per marionette for
complex moves (both from secondary description of the ASC piece, not a
verbatim quote confirmed against the saved HTML in this pass).

RELEVANCE: item 11 section 6's clearest counterexample to "strings must be
invisible to be convincing" -- Team America is proof that visible strings
do not by themselves break believability; timing, weight, and the puppet's
OWN motion physics (not string invisibility) carry the illusion, which is
directly relevant to this project's own visible/glowing-then-dark string
treatment (item 09's "strings should be dark, not glowing" recommendation).
