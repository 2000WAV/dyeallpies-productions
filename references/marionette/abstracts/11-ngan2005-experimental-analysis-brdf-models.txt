Ngan, A., Durand, F., and Matusik, W. (2005). Experimental Analysis of BRDF
Models. Eurographics Symposium on Rendering (EGSR) 2005, 117-126.

STATUS: full text saved (MERL technical report copy); see
papers/ngan2005-experimental-analysis-brdf-models.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: fits several standard analytic BRDF models (Blinn-Phong,
Cook-Torrance, Ward, Lafortune, He-Torrance-Sillion, etc.) to all 100
measured materials in the MERL database (Matusik et al. 2003, above) and
reports which analytic model best matches each material class, and how
well. Key qualitative finding directly relevant to item 4: a SINGLE
microfacet lobe fits glossy/matte materials well -- explicitly named:
"fabrics, rubber and paints" -- but HIGHLY SPECULAR materials (explicitly
named: "brushed metals" and "highly specular plastics") need a two-lobe
(coarse + fine) fit to capture both a broad glossy lobe and a narrow sharp
highlight; a single-lobe fit under- or over-blurs one of the two.

KEY NUMBERS: the paper's own fitted roughness/specular-exponent parameter
TABLE per material (the number this project would ideally quote, e.g. "gold
paint fits Cook-Torrance with roughness=X") is in a supplemental spreadsheet
(fit_param_with_errors.xls, linked from the Eurographics Digital Library
page) that was NOT fetched in this pass (spreadsheet, not a paper/PDF, and
outside this task's core scope) -- NOT FOUND as a directly quoted number
here; only the qualitative one-lobe-vs-two-lobe finding above is used.

RELEVANCE: supports item 4's claim that a lacquered/glossy surface needs
BOTH a broad soft highlight (the diffuse-ish "paint" lobe) and a separate,
narrower sharp highlight (the "highly specular plastic" second lobe) to
read as glossy/lacquered rather than merely bright -- directly informs the
recommended two-term specular design (a wide soft term plus a narrow sharp
term) if the marionette's material is pushed toward a glossier "lacquered
wood" look per the graphics-plan follow-up in HANDOFF.md.
