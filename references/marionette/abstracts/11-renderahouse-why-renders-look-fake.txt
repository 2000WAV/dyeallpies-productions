SUMMARY: a working-practitioner checklist of why CG renders/composites read
as fake, converging closely with several other items already in this
folder: (1) weak or missing CONTACT SHADOWS make an object look like it is
floating rather than resting/hanging in the scene -- explicitly named as
one of the most common and most fixable mistakes; recommends a TWO-PART
shadow (a broad soft ambient shadow plus a tighter, darker occlusion
shadow right at the actual contact point) rather than one uniform soft
shadow; (2) lighting/exposure mismatch between the CG element and the
plate; (3) over-perfect, noise-free CG surfaces next to a real photo's
natural imperfection/noise (converges with item 05 section 4.2's grain-
matching argument and this document's Rademacher 2001 citation on surface
smoothness).

KEY NUMBERS: none (qualitative practitioner checklist, not a study).

RELEVANCE: item 11 sections 3 and 6 -- the "floating object, no contact
shadow" failure mode is exactly the generic AR/VFX-insertion mistake this
project must avoid for the marionette hanging 30 cm off the wall; the
two-part shadow recommendation (broad soft + tight dark contact shadow)
gives a concrete, cheap structure for the shadow-catcher pass flagged as
"nice-to-have" in item 05 section 2.3, if it is built.
