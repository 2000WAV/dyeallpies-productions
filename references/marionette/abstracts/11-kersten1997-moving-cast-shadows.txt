SUMMARY (from the abstract): psychophysics using 3-D computer graphics
shows that a ball's perceived 3-D trajectory is determined almost entirely
by the motion of its CAST SHADOW, not by the ball's own on-screen path --
the "ball-in-a-box" demonstration: a ball on a fixed diagonal on-screen path
appears to slide along the floor when its shadow moves diagonally in
lockstep, but appears to RISE OFF THE FLOOR when the same ball's shadow
instead moves horizontally underneath it. Cast-shadow motion overrides
other strong depth cues and priors (constant object size, generic
viewpoint). The visual system assumes shadows come from a light source that
itself does not move, and misattributes shadow motion caused by a moving
light to object motion instead.

KEY NUMBERS: none quantitative in the abstract (the effect is reported as a
strong, qualitative illusion, not a percentage or threshold) -- NOT FOUND
for a numeric effect size from this abstract-only source.

RELEVANCE: the classical demonstration, alongside Wanger et al. 1992, that
a cast shadow is disproportionately load-bearing for a viewer's read of an
object's position/depth relative to a surface -- directly motivates why
the marionette's shadow on the wall (item 11 section 3, and item 05's own
penumbra-from-shadow recipe) matters more than almost any other single cue
for "the puppet is really there, 30 cm off the wall" rather than "pasted on
top of the footage."
