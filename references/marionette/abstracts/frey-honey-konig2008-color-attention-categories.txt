ABSTRACT (verbatim from PubMed):
"Certain locations attract human gaze in natural visual scenes. Are there
measurable features, which distinguish these locations from others? While
there has been extensive research on luminance-defined features, only few
studies have examined the influence of color on overt attention. In this
study, we addressed this question by presenting color-calibrated stimuli and
analyzing color features that are known to be relevant for the responses of
LGN neurons. We recorded eye movements of 15 human subjects freely viewing
colored and grayscale images of seven different categories. All images were
also analyzed by the saliency map model (L. Itti, C. Koch, & E. Niebur,
1998). We find that human fixation locations differ between colored and
grayscale versions of the same image much more than predicted by the
saliency map. Examining the influence of various color features on overt
attention, we find two extreme categories: while in rainforest images all
color features are salient, none is salient in fractals. In all other
categories, color features are selectively salient. This shows that the
influence of color on overt attention depends on the type of image. Also, it
is crucial to analyze neurophysiologically relevant color features for
quantifying the influence of color on attention."

RELEVANCE to item 9: colour (including saturation/colour-contrast) measurably
pulls eye movements beyond what a pure luminance-contrast saliency model
(Itti, Koch & Niebur 1998) predicts, but HOW MUCH depends on the surrounding
scene category -- directly supporting the brief's caveat that "a colour is
salient relative to its surround," i.e. there is no single hue that is
universally most salient independent of context; against a plain, warm,
off-white, low-clutter wall (this project's actual "scene category"), colour
contrast is not competing with other coloured clutter, so a saturated warm
hue should be comparatively MORE salient here than in a busy/colourful scene.
