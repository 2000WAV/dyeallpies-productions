SUMMARY: shape-from-shading (perceiving 3-D solidity purely from smooth
luminance gradients across a 2-D image, with no outline, texture, motion or
binocular cues) is shown to be an early, fast, "pop-out"-capable visual
process: (1) it relies on two built-in assumptions -- a SINGLE light source
for the whole scene, and that the light comes from ABOVE (in retinal, not
gravitational, coordinates); (2) shading-defined tokens support perceptual
grouping/segregation on their own; (3) detecting a single convex ("lit from
above, therefore bump") shape among concave distractors is a genuine
pop-out effect (reaction time flat vs. number of distractors) driven by the
shading gradient itself, not by low-level luminance-polarity differences;
(4) naive subjects find concave "dents" easier to pop out than convex
"bumps." Directly supports the item-9 brief's shading-cues claim: smooth
luminance gradients (simulating a single, consistent, from-above key light)
read as solid 3-D form pre-attentively and fast, independent of any object
outline -- i.e., the marionette's own body shading (not just its silhouette
edge) is what will sell "solid," and it needs one consistent, primarily-
overhead key light direction to avoid fighting the assumption the visual
system already applies.
