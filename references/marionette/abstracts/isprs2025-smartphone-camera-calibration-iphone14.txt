SUMMARY: Calibrates an iPhone 14 and two Google Pixel 7 units against four
calibration-grid configurations (small and large AprilGrid, small and large
checkerboard) and two distortion models (radial-tangential, equidistant) using
Kalibr, capturing at 1920x1080, 30 fps with the SensorLogger app. Reports the
standard deviation, i.e. uncertainty, of the recovered focal length and principal
point across the 24 configurations, roughly 1 to 2 pixels depending on grid,
model and phone, rather than the absolute fx and fy values themselves. Finds grid
size matters more than grid type or distortion model for focal-length stability,
and that different setup choices can shift the recovered focal length by 50-plus
pixels and the principal point by 35-plus pixels between configurations.

RELEVANCE: confirms that a real checkerboard or AprilGrid calibration of an
iPhone 14 at 1920x1080, 30 fps is a published, reproducible procedure, supporting
the use of a pinhole model with a specific fx in pixels for this project rather
than treating "phone camera" as unmeasurable; the roughly 1-2 pixel calibration
uncertainty, and the 50-plus pixel configuration-to-configuration spread, are a
useful error bar on any fx derived analytically from Apple's spec sheet instead
of an actual chessboard shoot of the same phone.
