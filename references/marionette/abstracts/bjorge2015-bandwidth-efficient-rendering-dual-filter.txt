Bjorge, M. (2015). Bandwidth-Efficient Rendering (dual-filter Kawase-style
blur/bloom). SIGGRAPH 2015 "Advances in Real-Time Rendering" course slides, ARM.

STATUS: full text saved (ARM community mirror), text-extracted; see
papers/bjorge2015-bandwidth-efficient-rendering-dual-filter.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: Presents dual filtering, a mobile-GPU-friendly reformulation of Kawase's
iterative blur (Kawase 2003) that alternates a small downsample-blur kernel while
progressively halving resolution, then an upsample-blur kernel while
progressively doubling resolution back up, minimizing texture bandwidth. A chain
of about five to six dual-filter passes approximates a very large-radius Gaussian
blur (the slides compare it to roughly a 35-pixel-radius Gaussian) at a fraction
of the cost, and is the technique behind Unity's and several mobile engines'
bloom implementations.

RELEVANCE: the practical bloom-blur implementation recommended for the neon
marionette's glow: cheap enough to run per-frame in a NumPy or offline
compositing pipeline (a handful of box or small-Gaussian downsample and upsample
passes) while giving a smooth, large-radius glow without the cost of a literal
large kernel.
