Lalonde, J.-F., and Efros, A.A. (2007). Using Color Compatibility for
Assessing Image Realism. IEEE 11th International Conference on Computer
Vision (ICCV 2007).

STATUS: full text saved (CMU Robotics Institute author copy); see
papers/lalonde2007-color-compatibility-realism.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: studies the color statistics of a large dataset of natural photos
and shows that composite images (an object pasted into a new background)
can be classified realistic-vs-not using ONLY color-distribution
compatibility between the pasted object and its new surroundings -- no
geometry, shading or matting quality is used. Three increasingly powerful
color models are compared (a global "universal" palette, then per-object
marginal and joint color histograms, then a joint-histogram-plus-texture
nearest-neighbor model).

KEY NUMBERS: classifier performance reported as ROC AUC (chance = 0.5):
0.59 (texture/texton-only, worst), 0.69-0.74 (marginal/joint color
histograms), up to 0.78-0.79 (joint histogram + texture matching, best
config) for distinguishing realistic from unrealistic composites purely
from color statistics.

RELEVANCE: quantifies, from an independent line of work, the same principle
item 09 already applied by picking pure red for figure-ground contrast: an
object's color relationship to its background measurably predicts whether
a composite "looks real," on the order of ROC AUC ~0.75-0.8 from color
alone -- i.e. getting the puppet's hue/warmth relationship to the off-white
wall right is not merely a salience trick (item 09), it is independently a
realism cue in the compositing literature.
