Mori, M. (1970/2012). The Uncanny Valley (translated by K.F. MacDorman and
N. Kageki). IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine, June 2012, 98-100.
(Original: Mori, M. (1970). Bukimi no tani [The Uncanny Valley]. Energy,
7(4), 33-35, in Japanese.)

STATUS: full text saved (MacDorman's own site, the authorized translation);
see papers/mori1970-2012-uncanny-valley.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: Mori's original essay proposing that affinity for a humanlike
robot/prosthetic/doll rises with human-likeness UP TO A POINT, then
plunges sharply into revulsion ("the uncanny valley") as it approaches but
fails to fully attain a lifelike appearance, before recovering once
human-likeness is indistinguishable from a real person. Uses the example of
a prosthetic hand: convincing at a glance, but a handshake revealing its
"limp, boneless grip... texture and coldness" collapses affinity into the
negative. Mori's own prescriptive conclusion (his final section) is that
since climbing all the way out of the valley to true human likeness is
extremely hard, robot/character designers should consider deliberately
STOPPING SHORT of high realism -- "I predict that it is possible to create
a safe level of affinity by deliberately pursuing a nonhuman design," using
the toy robot (partway up the FIRST hill, well before the valley) as the
model of a safe, appealing, clearly-non-human design.

KEY NUMBERS: none -- the essay is qualitative/conceptual, presenting the
now-famous affinity-vs-human-likeness curve without numeric axes; later
quantitative work (Mathur & Reichling 2016, above) is what supplies
measured curves.

RELEVANCE: the founding citation for section 1's uncanny-valley discussion,
and directly supports section 7's ranked advice: since the marionette is
already a stylized, non-photoreal wooden/painted figure rather than an
attempt at a realistic human face, Mori's own prescription argues FOR
staying in this stylized register (like his "toy robot," safely up the
first hill) rather than adding just enough realism (a detailed face, real
skin shading) to approach, without reaching, human likeness.
