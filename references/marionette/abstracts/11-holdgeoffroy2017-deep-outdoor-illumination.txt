J.-F. (2017). Deep Outdoor Illumination Estimation. CVPR 2017. arXiv:
1611.06403.

STATUS: full text saved (CVF open access); see

SUMMARY: a CNN fits the Hosek-Wilkie physically-based SKY model (sun
position, atmospheric turbidity, camera exposure) to a single outdoor LDR
photo. Not directly applicable to this project's INDOOR window-lit shot
(no sun/sky dome), but included per the brief and useful for calibrating
how accurate single-image light-DIRECTION estimation can get in the
easiest case (a single dominant, very bright, well-modeled light source --
closer in spirit to this shot's single dominant window key than a full
multi-light indoor scene is).

KEY NUMBERS:
- Sun angular position error: 80% of test images have combined
  (elevation+azimuth) error under 45 degrees.
- Sun ELEVATION error specifically: under 7 degrees for 80% of test images;
  camera field-of-view error under 11 degrees for 80% of test images.
- Azimuth precision "in an octant" (within 22.5 degrees) comparable to the
  prior state of the art (Lalonde et al.); precision "in a quadrant"
  (within 45 degrees) improved by roughly 10 PERCENTAGE POINTS over that
  same prior method.

RELEVANCE: even for the EASIEST single-light-source case (a bright outdoor
sun, well-modeled analytically), single-image estimation error is still
several degrees to tens of degrees -- reinforcing section 2's conclusion
that reading the window's direction directly from the plate's own visible
hand-shadow (item 05's approach) is likely more accurate for this specific
shot than a general-purpose learned estimator would be, precisely because
it uses this frame's own shadow evidence rather than a population prior.
