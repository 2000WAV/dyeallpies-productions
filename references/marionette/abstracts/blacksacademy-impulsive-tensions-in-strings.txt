SUMMARY: standard mechanics teaching material on what happens when a slack,
light, inextensible string suddenly becomes taut between two particles (or a
particle and a fixed/moving anchor) that are moving relative to each other. Because
the string is treated as perfectly inextensible and massless, going taut is modeled
exactly like a perfectly INELASTIC collision resolved ALONG the line of the
string: momentum is conserved along the string's direction, both ends of the
string are forced to the same velocity component along the string at that instant,
and kinetic energy IS lost in this component (analogous to a coefficient of
restitution of 0 along the string's axis) even though no material is literally
colliding -- only the velocity component perpendicular to the string is
unaffected. The size of the resulting impulse/tension spike is J = m_eff * v_along,
where v_along is the relative velocity component along the string just before it
snaps taut and m_eff is the reduced/effective mass of the system as seen along the
string.

RELEVANCE: this is the idealized-limit model (perfectly rigid string, instantaneous
snap, discrete energy loss) for the marionette drop in this project: the mannequin
falls freely for ~10-15 cm inside the fist, and the moment each of the 5 strings
reaches its slack length, that string's along-string velocity component is
killed nearly instantaneously, producing the "snap." A real string/tendon is not
perfectly rigid, so in a numerical (MuJoCo) simulation this idealized instantaneous
event becomes a brief but finite-stiffness impulsive event (see
mujoco-tendon-spatial-section-extract.txt and the notes on solref/solimp/timestep
in 02-puppet-body-dynamics.md) rather than a true discontinuity -- the finite
stiffness of the constraint solver is what turns the idealized "energy is simply
lost" collision into a "spring compresses then releases" bounce, which is also why
a small bounce after the snap is physically expected rather than a hypothesis.
