Livingstone, M., and Hubel, D. (1988). Segregation of Form, Color, Movement,
and Depth: Anatomy, Physiology, and Perception. Science, 240(4853), 740-749.

STATUS: full text saved (open PDF, author's Harvard Medical School lab page);
see papers/livingstone-hubel1988-segregation-form-color-movement-depth.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: the primate visual system splits early into a parvocellular
("colour and fine form") pathway and a magnocellular ("motion, depth,
coarse form") pathway; the magnocellular pathway is essentially colour-blind
(responds to luminance, not chromatic, contrast) but carries most of the
system's contrast sensitivity, motion, stereo-depth and fine spatial/temporal
resolution. Central demonstration (their Figure 1, described in the text,
p.~741): a set of yellow/blue-gray shapes drawn to be ISOLUMINANT (yellow and
"gray" patches matched in luminance, differing only in chromaticity) looks
FLAT and hard to parse as 3-D objects in normal (full-colour) viewing, but
springs into vivid apparent depth/3-D relief the moment it is viewed through
a blue filter -- which desaturates the colour difference and reveals a
genuine (previously colour-camouflaged) luminance difference between the
shapes. Their interpretation: form, depth and "3-D-ness" are carried
overwhelmingly by the luminance (parvo+magno-independent magnocellular)
channel; the colour channel alone, at equal luminance, gives weak or absent
form/depth information. Directly answers the item-9 brief's question "why
does chromatic contrast alone give weak shape" -- this is the primary
citation for that claim.
