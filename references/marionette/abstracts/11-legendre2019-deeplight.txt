LeGendre, C., Ma, W.-C., Fyffe, G., Flynn, J., Charbonnel, L., Busch, J.,
and Debevec, P. (2019). DeepLight: Learning Illumination for Unconstrained
Mobile Mixed Reality. CVPR 2019, 5918-5928. arXiv:1904.01175.

STATUS: full text saved (CVF open access); see
papers/legendre2019-deeplight.pdf/.txt.

SUMMARY: trains a network to infer HDR, omnidirectional illumination from a
single LDR, limited-FOV image captured by an ORDINARY MOBILE PHONE camera
(the exact capture modality relevant here) -- i.e. this is the closest of
the illumination-estimation papers to this project's actual hardware.
Training data comes from videos of reflective spheres (mirror, matte
silver, diffuse) held in the phone's frame, so the network learns to map
the visible background to plausible HDR lighting by matching how each
sphere type would look under the predicted lighting (a differentiable
image-based-relighting loss).

KEY NUMBERS: their best configuration (full loss with adversarial term)
achieves an RGB angular error on a rendered DIFFUSE test sphere of
approximately 9.8-10.8 degrees (indoor / outdoor unseen locations, n=450
each) -- i.e. even the best phone-camera single-frame illumination
estimate is typically off by roughly 10 degrees of light direction on a
diffuse test object. L1 image losses across mirror/diffuse/matte-silver
renderings are reported in the 0.12-0.17 range (arbitrary rendered-image
units, lower is better) across ablations.

RELEVANCE: gives an actual ERROR BAR (~10 degrees) for "how well can a
single phone photo alone tell you the room's lighting direction" -- useful
context for section 2's comparison between fully-automatic illumination
estimation and this project's existing item-05 approach of reading the
light's direction directly off the hand's own visible cast shadow, which
for a single, dominant, known light source (the lower-left window) should
comfortably beat this ~10-degree machine-learned baseline, since it uses
the SAME frame's own ground-truth shadow evidence rather than a learned
prior over lighting statistics.
