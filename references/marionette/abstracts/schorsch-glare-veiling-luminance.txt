SUMMARY: disability glare / veiling glare is caused by light scattered
within the eye (and, at the display/optics level, by a bright source
scattering into a bloom/halo around itself) forming a "veiling luminance"
that is added across the retinal image near the bright source, which
REDUCES local retinal contrast rather than adding a sharp edge. This is the
general-vision/lighting-design source for the item-9 claim that "a bright
glowing object on a bright ground loses its silhouette": bloom is, by
construction, veiling luminance -- it spreads some of the bright object's
energy onto the neighbouring (already-bright) wall pixels, which pulls the
object-vs-wall luminance ratio toward 1 (Weber contrast toward 0) exactly
where the eye is trying to read an edge. On a DARK background this same
spread is invisible (background stays near zero either way) which is why
bloom/rim-light conventions built for dark or neutral-grey backgrounds
(standard 3-point-lighting "rim light for separation" cinematography advice)
do not transfer to a bright background: there, spreading light toward the
background raises the background's own local luminance, undoing the
separation instead of creating it.

KEY NUMBERS: qualitative/definitional glossary entry; no numeric contrast
threshold given -- **NOT FOUND** for a percentage. The numeric case (bloom's
effect modelled as available light making the wall-side pixels near the
puppet brighter, reducing Weber contrast) is instead demonstrated with the
Michelson/Weber contrast arithmetic in data/09-colour-numbers.csv, using
this glossary only for the qualitative "veiling luminance reduces contrast"
mechanism name.
