SUMMARY: background on "Supermarionation," Gerry Anderson's marionette
technique used for Thunderbirds and related 1960s series. Puppets were
suspended and controlled by very fine metal filament wires that did DOUBLE
DUTY as both mechanical suspension/control lines AND electrical conductors,
carrying signals down to solenoid motors hidden in the puppets' heads that
opened/closed the mouth in sync with pre-recorded dialogue (the first
puppet production to use electronic lip-sync at this scale). The fineness
of the wire was itself a functional requirement (electrical + mechanical in
one), not purely a wish for invisibility, though thin wire is also harder
to see on screen than the thick control bars of a traditional stage
marionette.
